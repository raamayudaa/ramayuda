
# Someday //002
Today, I found myself trying to organize the future once again.

For the past few months, that has become a quiet routine. I read the same scholarship documents, revise the same statement of purpose, imagine the same university, then close everything before beginning again the following day. Curiously, the more carefully I prepare, the less certain the future appears. It does not become clearer. It merely acquires finer details to be anxious about.

I was about to call my mother.

Not because I had good news. Quite the opposite. I wanted to tell her that I had decided to pursue a master's degree. That I was willing to wager the next few years on something whose ending neither of us could possibly know. I cannot promise what work will come afterward. I cannot even promise that this is the safest path.

Before I could press the call button, my phone lit up.

It was my mother.

She asked, almost casually,

*"So... what are your plans for graduate school?"*

For a brief moment, I forgot that I had been searching for the right opening sentence.

Perhaps this is how mothers work. Somehow, they arrive at the very conversation you have been rehearsing in silence.

Our call lasted only a few minutes. We spoke about universities, applications, possibilities—most of them beginning with *maybe*. It is strange. I once believed adulthood would be filled with answers. Instead, it has introduced me to increasingly sophisticated forms of uncertainty.

There was one sentence I never managed to say.

Mom,

I am frightened.

Not of studying.

Not even of leaving.

But of discovering, years from now, that everything I am willing to risk today was never enough.

Still, I think there are journeys that never begin with certainty. They begin with the quiet decision to keep walking, despite the absence of it.

So tomorrow I will return to my application.

The documents will still be incomplete.

The future will remain undecided.

Yet tonight, one thing feels unexpectedly certain.

Before I could tell my mother about the future I had been carrying alone,

she had already been thinking about it.
